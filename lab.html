About

I am an undergraduate thesis student at the University of Toronto (St. George Campus) pursuing the Psychology Research Specialist Program and a major in Cognitive Science, advised by Dr. Morgan D. Barense. I expect to graduate in June 2027 and am applying to PhD programs in psychology, psychiatry, and neuroscience for entry in Fall 2027.

An aspiring cognitive neuroscientist, I am interested in generating a scientific account of consciousness. Drawing on converging lines of research in psychology and the computational, clinical, and systems neurosciences, I believe consciousness is the product of a synergy between mind and body. My ultimate research aim is to use altered states of consciousness, such as psychedelic experiences, dream states, epilepsy, and mystical experiences as a vantage point from which to carve consciousness at its joints.

Beyond research aspirations, I am a diligent individual with a skillset in academic writing, data analysis, and graphic design. Outside the lab, I train karate, cycle, and practice yoga. Please feel free to contact me if we share interests!

Research

Ongoing Projects

Thesis: Behavioural and Intracranial Study

Determining the mechanisms of temporal context recovery in autobiographical memory

Lei, D., Barense, M.D., Seger, S., Lohnas, L., & Lega, B.

Data collection · October 2026

Retrieved-context theories predict that recalling a memory reinstates its original context, biasing recall toward temporally nearby events — but this has only been tested in controlled lab settings. This project tests these predictions in real-world autobiographical memory using HippoCamera, first in healthy adults, then in epilepsy patients implanted with the NeuroPace RNS system to probe the underlying neural mechanisms.

Get in touch about this study →

Sleep / TMR study

Investigating the role of sleep in episodic memory consolidation using a naturalistic memory paradigm

Lei, D., Zhang, X., Rafla, D., Zhang, L., & Levine, B.

Data collection · August 2026

Sleep is known to support memory consolidation, but most evidence comes from lab tasks like wordlist recall rather than naturalistic memories. This project examines how sleep affects free recall of a naturalistic experience — a controlled hospital tour — and uses targeted memory reactivation (TMR) during sleep to probe the underlying mechanisms.

Qualitative Study

A digital educational program to improve memory for older adults

Lei, D., Hong, B., Hughes, E. C., & Barense, M. D. (2025)

Manuscript writing · August 2026

HippoCamera, a smartphone app shown to improve memory for everyday events, has driven requests for content explaining the science behind it. This project developed a complementary educational program covering key memory strategies, and interviewed older adults to identify what drives engagement with technology-based memory tools — informing how future interventions can be designed.

Theoretical paper

A Daoist Perspective of Consciousness

Lei, D. & Djedovic, A. (2025) · Canadian Undergraduate Journal of Cognitive Science, student-reviewed

This paper proposes an embodied account of consciousness that integrates ancient Chinese philosophy and medicine with predictive processing theories from cognitive science. It introduces the synergistically affectively interoceptively synchronizing mind-body hypothesis (SAIS), drawing parallels between predictive processing and the concept of Qi to suggest consciousness emerges from a dynamic, exaptive relationship between mind and body.

Review article

From ‘Ha Ha!’ to ‘Aha!’: Towards an Integrated Understanding of the Effect of Humour on Insight Problem-Solving

Lei, D. (2026) · Mentis: Journal of Cognitive Science and Artificial Intelligence, student-reviewed

Insight problem-solving research has long focused on cognitive flexibility as the key driver of the ‘Aha!’ experience, with little attention paid to humour as a contributing factor. This paper reviews emerging evidence that humour meaningfully facilitates insight, arguing for its inclusion in how we define and study insight problem-solving.

Poster presentation · Best Undergraduate Poster Award

Determining the Mechanisms of Temporal Context Recovery in Autobiographical Memory with Ambulatory Intracranial Recording

Lei, D., Barense, M. D., Seger, S., Lohnas, L., & Lega, B. (2026) · Ontario Student Neuroscience Conference (OSNC)

Retrieved-context theory predicts that recalling an event reinstates its temporal context, yet this has only been tested in the laboratory. This poster reports the behavioural phase of the thesis, which tests the prediction in real-world autobiographical memory with HippoCamera, and sets out the planned ambulatory intracranial recordings in patients implanted with the NeuroPace RNS system.

Poster presentation

Unravelling the Relationship Between Mental Health, Vection, and Hearing Loss: A Lifespan Approach

Lei, D., Hong, L., & Campos, J. L. (2026) · UHN Summer Training and Research (STAR) Program Research Day

Older adults with age-related hearing loss (ARHL) fall three times more often, and overlap between the auditory and vestibular systems may link hearing to balance. Over-reliance on vision can amplify vection, the illusory sense of self-motion, and anxiety and depression may shape that experience. Using a global visual motion paradigm, this study asks how vection differs with age, hearing status, and mental health.

Poster presentation

The “High” Mind: Towards a Unified Theoretical Account of Insight Problem-Solving, Predictive Processing & Relevance Realization Through Oneirogenic Psychedelics

Lei, D. & Vervaeke, J. (2025) · Psychology Undergraduate Research Community Symposium, University of Toronto

Psychedelics have long been thought to promote insight problem-solving, and recent work suggests psychedelic and dream states share a neural basis that may help the brain generalize and avoid overfitting. This paper examines how psychedelics could facilitate relevance realization and predictive processing, drawing on their effects on insight, the overfitted brain hypothesis, and the REBUS model.
